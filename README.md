We present SMORPHILA, an open-source Python software for the acquisition and preprocessing of
landmark-based morphometric data from two-dimensional images. In addition to standard
landmark-acquisition functions, SMORPHILA allows the geometric standardization of articulated
structures by rotating segments to predefined orientations while preserving their lengths and
connectivity. As a case study, we applied SMORPHILA to the analysis of limb morphology in two
sympatric Malagasy frogs, Spinomantis aglavei and S. phantasticus. Traditional and geometric
morphometric analyses produced highly concordant results. They identified body size as the
principal source of morphological variation, showed similar allometric patterns, and detected
consistent shape differences between species. However, the geometric approach provided a more
informative description of morphological variation. By preserving the spatial relationships among
anatomical structures, it described shape change as a coordinated transformation of the entire
organism rather than as a collection of independent changes in individual measurements. This
approach also allowed to distinguish more clearly between allometric and species-specific
components of variation. These results highlight the value of extending the geometric morphometric
approach to articulated appendages and identify SMORPHILA as a practical tool for facilitating its
application to these structures.
